# Startup Fundraising Readiness

## Summary
Even a short founder-facing checklist makes one durable point: fundraising readiness is not just pitch quality but document quality. Before investors begin diligence, founders benefit from having core legal, ownership, and incentive records organized enough that questions about incorporation, equity, IP ownership, confidentiality, and team-exit mechanics do not stall the process. [src-2026-06-10-015]

## Key Ideas
- Fundraising readiness includes governance and ownership clarity, not just storytelling. The checklist foregrounds founder agreements, shareholder agreements, and a cap table as basic artifacts investors will care about. [src-2026-06-10-015]
- Team-structure risks matter early. A co-founder exit clause and explicit IP assignment both reduce ambiguity around what happens if contributors leave or if ownership is disputed. [src-2026-06-10-015]
- Incentive and confidentiality infrastructure also matter. ESOP documentation and NDAs appear as part of the same readiness surface rather than as isolated legal chores. [src-2026-06-10-015]
- This source is best read as a checklist starter, not complete legal advice, because it does not explain jurisdiction, financing stage, or which documents are optional versus essential in specific contexts. [src-2026-06-10-015]
