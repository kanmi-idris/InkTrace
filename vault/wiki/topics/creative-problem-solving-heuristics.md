# Creative Problem Solving Heuristics

## Summary
Claude Shannon's "Creative Thinking" talk treats creative work as a set of trainable problem-solving moves rather than a mystical flash of inspiration. The core picture is that strong work needs both capability and drive, but that concrete heuristics such as simplification, analogy, reframing, decomposition, generalization, and inversion can materially improve how often useful solutions appear. [src-2026-06-10-014]

## Key Ideas
- Creative output is not explained by talent alone. Shannon argues that training, above-average ability, and motivation all matter, with motivation covering curiosity, constructive dissatisfaction, and delight in elegant results. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- Simplification is a first-class strategy: strip away non-essential detail until the problem becomes legible, solve the smaller core if possible, then add refinements back. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- Analogy search reduces cognitive jump distance. Instead of inventing from scratch, find a similar solved problem and map its structure back onto the current one. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- Reframing the same problem in different words or viewpoints helps break mental ruts and can expose constraints or symmetries that were previously invisible. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- Generalization is useful both after and during solution discovery: once a narrow answer appears, test whether the same principle applies to a broader class of problems. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- Structural decomposition matters when the solution is too far away in one leap. Introduce intermediate subproblems or subsidiary results that make the path traversable. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- Inversion can turn a stubborn forward problem into an easier backward one by assuming the desired result and reasoning in reverse toward the givens. [src-2026-06-10-014]

## Contradictions
- The strongest reusable material is Shannon's method list, but some surrounding framing, especially his remarks about IQ thresholds, reflects the language and assumptions of a 1952 research talk rather than a modern evidence-grounded theory of creativity. [src-2026-06-10-014]
