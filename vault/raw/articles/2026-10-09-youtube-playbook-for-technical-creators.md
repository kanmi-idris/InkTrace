# YouTube Playbook for Technical Creators

## Source scope and provenance
This page presents a practical playbook for developers who make YouTube videos. The page says its packaging advice comes from Theo's September 2026 masterclass. It says the rest comes from five strategy and editing calls with a developer-education channel in September and October 2026. Tips marked “Theo” are attributed to that masterclass. The page says it was updated 2026-10-08. The page does not name its author or the developer-education channel in the captured text.

## Main recommendations
- Raise the floor. Improve the weakest videos before making bigger bets. Package the idea before recording. The thumbnail should stop a scroll; the title should earn a click.
- Aim for a “stretch” title: more intriguing than the literal description, but still fulfilled by the video. Avoid promises the video cannot pay off.
- Write the first 15–20 seconds word for word. Confirm the click, show why the topic matters, state what viewers will get, then reach useful content quickly.
- For growth videos aimed at new viewers, use re-engagements near 1, 3, and 6 minutes. Deliver early value. Put slower context and deeper material later. The page says deep dives for existing viewers can use a different shape.
- End inside the last useful point. Avoid a long separate outro.
- The page proposes one long-form video per week and videos over eight minutes as a starting cadence. It also says cadence depends on production effort and that creators may need to choose which of teaching, showing, or entertaining to do well.
- Run a strategy for 4–6 months before replacing it. Adjust it based on results.

## Workflow and editing
- Before recording, decide the viewer's promise, draft the title and thumbnail, write the intro, outline the framework and main points, and verify every page or example that will appear on screen.
- Record alternate intros, clean retakes, modular endings, optional back-half sections, and short versions of long explanations.
- Edit in three passes: structure, full watch-through for flow, then polish and graphics. The page recommends checking cuts for context and energy, and showing screen proof early.
- Use graphics and motion only when they communicate an idea. The page says the editor owns execution and craft, while the creator has final approval.
- Publish with a short description and links. The page recommends sharing on X and Discord and reviewing opening retention during the first week.

## Strategy and packaging
- The page contrasts broad reach, a specific niche, and passion-project content. Each offers different trade-offs in views, advertiser fit, reach, and predictability.
- It recommends creating for the audience the creator wants, while retaining a distinct voice. It warns against chasing a viral audience that does not fit the channel.
- For titles, it distinguishes literal “truth,” a deliverable “stretch,” and clickbait. For thumbnails, it recommends a clear idea, one or two consistent face positions, minimal text, and a readable concept at phone size. These are the page's recommendations, not tested universal rules.
- It suggests repeating a successful format, spaced about monthly. It says a follow-up format is worth repeating if it gets roughly 85–90% of the original video's views.

## Retention and algorithm claims
- The page describes recommendations as driven by overlapping watch histories. It claims the system uses recent watch history and says tags and categories are not used. It attributes several such claims to Theo.
- The page recommends giving average view duration more weight than click-through rate, reading the first six minutes of the retention graph, and repackaging videos that have flatlined.
- It says satisfaction signals are not publicly known and labels its own explanation as a working guess. Treat claims about watch-history size, tags, subscriber impact, and the recommendation system as third-party statements, not official YouTube documentation.
- It recommends testing a new title or thumbnail after 48–72 hours for some videos, but says not to change a video that is still performing. It says A/B testing may become meaningful around 1,000–10,000 impressions per day and explicitly calls this a rough VidIQ estimate, not a significance threshold.

## Limits
This page is a synthesis of cited creator and team advice. It is not an official YouTube guide or a controlled study. Many metrics, timings, thresholds, and algorithm descriptions are heuristics. The page itself says its core checklist targets growth videos for new viewers and gives exceptions for deep dives, technical audiences, and production limits.
