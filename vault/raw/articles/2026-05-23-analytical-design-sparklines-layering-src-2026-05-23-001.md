The six principles of analytical design captured from the supplied note:

1. show comparisons, contrasts, and differences
2. show causality, mechanism, structure, and explanation
3. show multivariate data rather than over-reducing to one or two variables
4. completely integrate words, numbers, images, and diagrams
5. thoroughly describe the evidence
6. analytical presentations ultimately depend on the quality, relevance, and integrity of their content

Sparklines section captured from the supplied note:

- Sparklines are described as:
  - word-sized
  - data-intense
  - typographic-resolution graphics
- The note emphasizes:
  - inline embedding with prose or tables
  - minimal ornamentation
  - value in showing trend shape rather than exact readings
- The note also gives practical design heuristics:
  - roughly x-height scale
  - short horizontal length
  - optional endpoint or anomaly markers
  - pairing with a current numeric value

Layering and separation section captured from the supplied note:

- The note frames layering as a key way to let dense information coexist in one space.
- Techniques listed include:
  - hierarchy by weight or value
  - distinct hue for separation rather than decoration
  - keeping grids and scaffolding visually quiet
  - reducing phantom visual effects such as the “1+1=3” line illusion

Micro/macro section captured from the supplied note:

- The note distinguishes displays that communicate:
  - an overall pattern at a glance
  - fine detail on close inspection
- It positions this as a way to avoid choosing between overview and detail.

Escaping flatland section captured from the supplied note:

- The note argues that flat media can still encode multiple information dimensions through:
  - color
  - size
  - shape
  - position
  - time
  - layering
- It explicitly rejects 3D chart gimmicks that distort proportion without adding useful information.

Range-frame and dot-dash plot section captured from the supplied note:

- Range-frame:
  - replace a full axis with a line spanning the actual data range
- Dot-dash plot:
  - rework scatterplot axes into marginal distributions or rug-like summaries
- The general principle is that standard chart furniture should be redesigned to carry information where possible.

Confections, parallelism, narrative, and causality sections captured from the supplied note:

- `Confections` are described as explanatory assemblages combining images, text, diagrams, and maps in one composition.
- `Parallelism` is framed as repeating visual structure to enable comparison.
- Narrative graphics of space and time are highlighted as displays that combine multiple dimensions in one frame.
- Causality guidance emphasizes showing intervention, response, and mechanism together when possible.
