Conceptual framing captured from the supplied text:

- The article presents magnet links as an alternative to traditional `.torrent` files.
- It explains that traditional torrent files contain:
  - tracker information
  - metadata about shared files
  - integrity-check information
  - possibly distributed hash table (`DHT`) information
- The supplied text says magnet links strip this down to the essential information needed to reconstruct the swarm.

Magnet-link details captured from the supplied text:

- The article says a magnet link is essentially a string of text.
- It states that a magnet link contains:
  - a unique identifier
  - parameters depending on the torrent
  - a cryptographic hash of the torrent files
- The supplied explanation says peers can compare hashes to find matching torrents and reconstruct the swarm, which the article explicitly relates to trackerless DHT-style discovery.

Advantages captured from the supplied text:

- Magnet links are links rather than files, so they can be embedded directly in webpages and messaging flows.
- The article says this removes the need to host and download a separate `.torrent` file.
- The supplied text also argues that magnet links reduce malware risk associated with downloading files.
- Another explicit claim is robustness: as long as a seeder still exists and the hash can identify the swarm, a magnet link can still function even if the original contributors or file-hosting setup are gone.

Usage notes captured from the supplied text:

- Using a magnet link is described as clicking the link or pasting it into a BitTorrent client that supports magnet-link addresses.
- The rest of the process is described as happening automatically once the client resolves the swarm from the hash and discovery network.
