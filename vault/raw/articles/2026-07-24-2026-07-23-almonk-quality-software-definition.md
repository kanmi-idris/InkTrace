# What Is Quality Software? — Adam Monk (@almonk)

**URL:** https://x.com/almonk/status/2079461952577802549?s=20
**Captured:** 2026-07-23

Adam Monk (worked at an AI company for ~3 years) publishes observations on software quality in the age of AI-generated code. His core argument: as the bar to entry drops, quality drops with it, and that is everyone's problem.

## Opening Position

- AI makes software easier to produce, which accelerates both good and bad output
- Calling everything AI-made "slop" is too easy — humans wrote bad software first
- The problem is AI does it faster, at greater volume

## Four Pillars of Quality Software

### 1. Quality software does not break
- This is the starting line — polish, taste, and craft sit downstream of this fact
- Software that breaks is not quality software, however handsome it looks
- It works offline, on your phone, when your connection hangs, when your battery dies
- You never notice any of this

### 2. Quality software does not demand attention
- The best software is quiet — it does not announce itself, interrupt, or ask for admiration
- Loud software (badges, banners, tours of features) correlates with the loudest marketing
- A tool confident in its work does not keep reminding you it exists

### 3. Quality software knows its limits
- **Limits of size**: every megabyte and button is a claim on machine and attention — restraint is respect
- **Limits of purpose**: what does this software do? Software that tries to do everything does nothing reliably
- Companies racing to "be agentic" are forgetting why users used them in the first place
- "The slop-creep is real now that features are easier to create than ever"

### 4. Quality software fixes fast
- Nothing is unbreakable — the test is what happens next
- Break is found, fix is pushed, fix arrives without ceremony
- Updates come, do their work, and leave you where you were, only slightly better off
- "You should not have to attend to your own tools"

## Closing: AI Needs Boundaries

- Quality comes down to software that respects the user
- The boundary that matters most: writing that a human is expected to read
- "Let the agents build the software. The words we put in front of people should be our own."
- Slop in writing is not a quality problem — it is a respect problem
