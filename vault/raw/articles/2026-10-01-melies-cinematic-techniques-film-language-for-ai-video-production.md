## User-provided text

If you're producing videos with artificial intelligence or planning to do so, pay a visit to this website. Because the way to create a good AI video isn't just about gathering good prompts from here and there—it's about understanding the language of cinema.

This website, called "Cinematic Techniques," brings together a full 424 different techniques used in film and video production. Even under the heading of camera movements alone, there are 86 different techniques: Dolly In / Out, Tracking Shot, FPV Drone, 360-Degree Orbit, Dolly Zoom, Handheld...

But the truly valuable part is that this website isn't just a dictionary of cinema terms. In these pages, embedded within an AI video production service, topics like composition, camera angles, lighting, lenses, color tones, transitions, and genre-specific storytelling forms like Film Noir are covered systematically. Moreover, each technique is presented with;

→ How it's used  
→ Its effect on the image  
→ Video examples  
→ Reference images  
→ Prompts that can be used in AI video production.

In my opinion, the real potential lies not so much in copying the hundreds of prompts here, but in transforming this resource into an AI-supported video production system.

For example:  
"I want to create an unsettling and eerie scene."  
↓  
Determine the appropriate shooting technique.  
↓  
Create a Camera Angle × Lighting × Lens × Color combination.  
↓  
Define the scene's visual language.  
↓  
Generate a prompt suitable for the AI video tool you'll use.

You could even build a Skill with this logic, or feed the site's content to Claude Opus 5.5 and use it like a cinematographer.  
When we look at it this way, the real skill in AI video production is shifting: Not memorizing prompts, but being able to describe what you want in visual language.  
Because as AI tools evolve, the technical part of writing prompts will become increasingly easier. But knowing which camera movement evokes which emotion, how light alters the narrative, and how lens choice affects the distance between the viewer and the character will remain a human creative decision.

In the age of AI video, what we need to learn isn't more prompts—it's more cinema language.
