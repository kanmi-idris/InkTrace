Core framing captured from the supplied article:

- The article contrasts “common OTP” with “reverse OTP.”
- Common OTP is described as server-generated and delivered to the user through SMS, email, or similar channels.
- Reverse OTP is described as user-generated and then sent from the user side to the server for verification.

Mechanics captured from the supplied article:

- The user generates an OTP on their phone or SIM.
- The server validates the user-generated code.
- Verification succeeds only if timing, keying, device, or SIM-related checks pass.

Benefits claimed in the supplied article:

- Reduced reliance on centralized SMS or email gateways.
- Lower server-side messaging costs.
- A stronger tie between verification and the user’s phone or SIM.
- Potentially better resilience against delivery delays and some server-side messaging failures.
- Applicability to fintech, e-commerce, gaming, SaaS, and similar use cases.

Challenges captured from the supplied article:

- Reverse OTP is described as more complex to implement than a standard OTP flow.
- Cross-device compatibility and UX are presented as non-trivial concerns.
- The article proposes solutions such as SMS QR flows for desktop and mobile intent-based flows on phones.

Security notes captured from the supplied article:

- The article claims Reverse OTP can be as secure or more secure than standard OTP in some threat models.
- It still emphasizes that device integrity, encryption, and fraud monitoring remain necessary.
