# Vaginal Candida and Sexual Contact: General Health Information

## Evidence status
This is a user-provided health-information thread. It is not a diagnosis or treatment plan. The claims are not independently verified here. Persistent, severe, recurrent, or unclear symptoms require assessment by a qualified healthcare professional.

## Main points in the thread
- Vaginal Candida infection is not usually classified as a sexually transmitted infection.
- Candida can normally live on the skin and in the body. An overgrowth can cause symptoms.
- Sexual contact does not automatically cause infection in the male partner.
- Some men may develop penile irritation, itching, redness, soreness, or a rash.
- Risk may be higher with diabetes, lack of circumcision, or other conditions linked to yeast overgrowth.
- Friction can worsen symptoms in a partner with already inflamed vaginal tissue. The thread advises waiting until symptoms improve.
- An asymptomatic male partner usually does not need automatic antifungal treatment. Treatment should follow symptoms or clinical advice.
- Vaginal discharge and itching can also result from bacterial vaginosis, sexually transmitted infections, or other conditions. Repeated self-treatment can delay correct diagnosis.
- Candida does not prove infidelity or an STI. Possible contributing factors include antibiotics, hormonal changes, and changes in the vaginal environment.

## Safety boundary
Symptoms can overlap across different infections and conditions. Avoid assuming Candida from symptoms alone. Seek clinical testing or assessment when symptoms are severe, recurrent, persistent, or not improving with appropriate care. Consider STI testing when exposure or symptoms make it relevant.
