# California CIPA provisions and session-replay risk

Captured: 2026-11-04
Primary statutes: California Penal Code §§ 631 and 637.2
Sources:
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=631.
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=637.2
Legal status context: https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/09/privacy-litigation-report-takeaways-from-august-2026-decisions

Section 631 addresses unauthorized interception and reading of communications in transit, including conduct without all parties' consent. Section 637.2 permits an injured person to seek the greater of $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages, plus injunctive relief. The statute does not say “$5,000 per session.”

Session replay and similar website tracking have generated CIPA lawsuits. A September 2026 legal update describes courts continuing to allow some §631 website-tracking claims while other decisions reject or limit different CIPA theories. The outcomes depend on the alleged data, timing, consent, vendor role, and legal theory. A session replay tool is not automatically unlawful just because it records interaction data.

California SB 690, signed September 30, 2026, limits private actions under §638.51 for certain website, online-app, and mobile-app pen-register claims. It does not amend §631's wiretap provision. This legislation concerns a separate CIPA theory and should not be conflated with session-replay wiretap claims.
