# CIA Reading Improvement Course

The CIA released a "Reading Improvement Course" hidden from the public since 1955. 117 pages. Key quotes:

1) "Good reading is not necessarily rapid reading. The rate must be adjusted to comprehension requirements."

2) "To read well you must concentrate. No activity except perhaps writing or thinking makes more rigorous demands upon the whole mind than reading."

3) "Suggestions to develop your ability to concentrate: Be interested. There is no spur to concentration like honest interest."

4) Some readers jot down key thoughts as they read. Underlining key ideas and making notes at the end of each section serves the same purpose.

5) "Concentration is a habit and must be formed. Habits require, at first, a conscious effort of the will."

6) "When our attention wanders, words slip by us and leave no trace of meaning."

7) "Drawing conclusions is the final step of the reading process and should be based on sound judgment."

8) Three types of reading:
- Intensive reading: master new subject material. Procedure: (a) rapid preliminary survey, (b) formulate questions, (c) careful reading, (d) review and recitation.
- Extensive reading: acquire broader frame of reference. As fast as the thinking process permits.
- Scanning: main idea, specific information, preview.

9) SQAR3 Reading Strategy:
- Scan: introductory paragraph, subtitles, key sentences, summary
- Question: turn titles into questions
- Anticipate: try to answer mentally before reading
- Read: entire selection for author's ideas
- Recite: close book, tell yourself in own words
- Review: gist of article in own words

10) How to read with purpose: have definite questions, make mental notes of main points, stop to think over material, notice illustrations, develop habit of criticizing what you read.

"Reading is a pleasure, a source of information, and the basis for most decisions that are made today. It deserves the liveliest interest and the best techniques we can master."
