# Counter-Offer as Market-Value Evidence: Resignation Case Study

## Evidence status
This is a user-provided social-media thread. The employee, employer, offers, and timeline are not independently verified. Treat the lessons as the author's viewpoint, not universal employment advice.

## Case summary
An employee resigned after accepting a new offer. The manager used four retention tactics:
1. A counter-offer below the new salary.
2. A proposed promotion.
3. An Africa expansion project that the employee had requested earlier.
4. Personal loyalty and guilt about the team.

The managing director then called with a fifth offer. The reported offer included a higher salary, a signing bonus, and a car. The employee rejected it, forwarded the written offer to the new employer, and received a higher matching offer. He kept the documents and later used the former employer's final written offer as evidence for his market value during a salary review.

## Reported lesson structure
- The market may pay more than an existing employer pays until the employer faces a resignation.
- A counter-offer can signal that the employer could have paid more earlier.
- The author advises against accepting counter-offers because trust may change and the employee may be treated as a flight risk.
- Written offers, promises, and counter-offers can document compensation evidence for future negotiations.

These lessons are anecdotal. Counter-offer outcomes depend on the employer, role, manager, contract, and reason for leaving.

Three lessons.

One. The market pays what the market pays. Your current employer is only willing to match it when they are about to lose you. That means they always knew.

Two. Never accept a counter-offer. The trust is broken. You are now the flight risk they will replace within 9 months.

Three. Document everything. The counter-offer is not the goal. It is the receipt.
