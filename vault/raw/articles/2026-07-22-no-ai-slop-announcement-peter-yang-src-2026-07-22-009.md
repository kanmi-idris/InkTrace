## Summary

Open-sourcing his personal AI skill that removes 20+ patterns of AI slop from any piece of writing. Uses it to edit everything from emails to docs.

## Usage

1. **"/no-ai-slop (text)"** — edit writing, removes slop patterns and lists what it removed
2. **"/no-ai-slop is this slop? (text)"** — detect AI writing, lists all slop patterns found
3. **"draft an AI slop post (text)"** — generate satire/cringe slop

## 10 Slop Patterns Listed

1. Binary contrasts ("It's not X. It's Y.")
2. Throat-clearing openers ("Here's the thing," "Let me be clear")
3. Faux-insight setups ("What nobody tells you," "The part everyone misses")
4. Colon reveals ("The best part: it learns.")
5. Dramatic fragments ("That's it. That's the whole thing.")
6. Superficial analysis ("highlighting the team's commitment to innovation")
7. Importance puffery ("marks a pivotal moment," "a testament to")
8. Weasel attribution ("experts agree," "studies show")
9. Synonym cycling ("The agent handles your email. The assistant drafts replies.")
10. Fake-profound endings ("The future isn't coming. It's already here.")

## 25/50/25 Rule for Editing with AI

1. **First 25%** — human starts by voice dictating or writing a rough draft without AI
2. **Middle 50%** — uses /newsletter and /no-ai-slop skills to clean up the draft (grammar, clarity, etc.)
3. **Last 25%** — human manually goes through piece line by line without AI

Applies to building with AI too: first 25% (taste to shape requirements), middle 50% (AI writes code), last 25% (validate the work).

## Why Slop Persists

- Easier to give AI feedback to edit than do it manually
- Easier to be lazy without AI (e.g., less productive on long flights)
- Pumping out slop can drive real results (some creators publish 10 AI slop posts/hour)

## Trust Argument

"People who know AI can see the slop coming from a mile away." A post can perform while still costing trust with people whose judgment matters.

Examples:
- Brian Chesky's hacked account posting AI-generated thread (readers spotted it immediately)
- Alex Cohen's letter to employees about slop putting unfair burden on the reader
