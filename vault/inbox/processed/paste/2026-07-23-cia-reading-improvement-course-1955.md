# CIA Reading Improvement Course (1955) — 10 Key Quotes & Techniques

**Captured:** 2026-07-23

A 117-page CIA reading course that had been hidden from the public since 1955. Covers reading comprehension, concentration, speed, and systematic reading strategies.

## 10 Key Quotes

1. "Good reading is not necessarily rapid reading. The rate must be adjusted to comprehension requirements."

2. "To read well you must concentrate. No activity except perhaps writing or thinking makes more rigorous demands upon the whole mind than reading."

3. "Suggestions to develop your ability to concentrate: Be interested. There is no spur to concentration like honest interest."

4. "Some readers have found certain devices useful in keeping their minds on the subject. One device is to jot down key thoughts as one reads. These words act as a fence to confine our wandering thoughts. The practice of underlining key ideas and making notes at the end of each section serves the same purpose."

5. "Concentration is a habit and must be formed. Habits require, at first, a conscious effort of the will. Force yourself to let nothing come between your mind and the matter on the page before you."

6. "When our attention wanders, words slip by us and leave no trace of meaning. Under such conditions we cannot expect a harvest of logical thought."

7. "Drawing conclusions is the final step of the reading process and should be based on sound judgment of the author's qualifications, facts presented, methods of presentation, validity of arguments, or style of presentation."

8. **Three types of reading:**
   - **Intensive reading**: master new subject material — translate, integrate, analyze, evaluate. Procedure: (a) rapid survey, (b) formulate questions, (c) careful reading, (d) review and recite important points
   - **Extensive reading**: broader frame of reference — rapid and thorough, as fast as thinking permits
   - **Scanning**: main idea, specific information, preview of material

9. **The SQAR3 Reading Strategy:**
   - **Scan** — quickly scan intro paragraph, subtitles, key sentences, keywords, summary
   - **Question** — turn title or key sentences into questions
   - **Anticipate** — before reading, try to answer questions mentally
   - **Read** — read entire selection looking for author's ideas
   - **Recite** — close the book, tell yourself in your own words what you learned; write notes
   - **Review** — later, review the gist in your own words

10. **How to read with purpose:**
    - Have definite questions before reading
    - Make mental note of main points while reading
    - Stop periodically to think over material
    - Notice illustrations and examples
    - Develop habit of criticizing what you read

## Closing Quote

"Reading is a pleasure, a source of information, and the basis for most decisions that are made today. It deserves the liveliest interest and the best techniques we can master."
