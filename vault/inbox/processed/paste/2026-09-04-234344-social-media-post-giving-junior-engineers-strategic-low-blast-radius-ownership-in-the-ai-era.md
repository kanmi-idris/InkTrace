# Social Media Post: Giving Junior Engineers Strategic, Low-Blast-Radius Ownership in the AI Era

## Source overview
A short, unsourced social-media opinion post (no external link provided) arguing that because AI tools have absorbed most of the 'tactical' day-to-day coding work junior engineers traditionally cut their teeth on, junior engineers instead need deliberate exposure to strategic, higher-ownership work, but in a setting where mistakes are survivable rather than catastrophic.

## The argument, summarized
The post's core claim is that AI has displaced 'tactical programming' (implied to mean routine, well-specified coding tasks), so the traditional path by which juniors built judgment through repeated small tactical decisions no longer functions the same way. Its proposed alternative is to deliberately hand juniors a meaningfully large, real chunk of ownership, but scoped to a project that is low-stakes without being zero-stakes, with an internal tool suggested as a natural fit because AI-assisted development has made such internal projects cheaper to justify building at all. The post recommends giving juniors the same AI tooling budget as senior engineers on that work, encouraging them to move and fail quickly, and then doing a structured post-mortem with them when failures occur, with the explicit intent that lessons from that failure should also be pulled upward and shared with the rest of the engineering organization, not just kept with the individual.

The post's own one-sentence summary of this philosophy: hire juniors, give them work that matters, let them fail, then help them recover from it.
