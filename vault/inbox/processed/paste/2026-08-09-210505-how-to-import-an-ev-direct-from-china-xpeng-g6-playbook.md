# How to Import an EV Direct from China: XPeng G6 Playbook

## Source overview
This 09 Aug 2026 article describes an author's personal process for buying a China-spec 2025 XPeng G6 directly from China for use in Nigeria. The author presents it as a personal record, not a sponsored endorsement or universal import guide. Shipping and clearing were still in progress when the article was published.

## Why the author chose an EV
The author owns a 2014 Toyota Corolla L and reports rising fuel costs. The author's off-grid solar setup reportedly generates close to one megawatt-hour per month, making EV charging practical for the author's use case.

The author considered a Toyota RAV4, Tesla Model Y, BYD Atto 3, and BYD Sealion 7 before choosing the XPeng G6. The reported selection criteria were compact size, practical driving, English support, low mileage, and ground-clearance considerations for Nigerian roads.

## Reported purchase workflow
1. Identify a vehicle on AutoCango and reserve quickly because inventory moves fast. The author reserved a low-mileage 2025 XPeng G6 display car with a reported $500 fee paid through USDT.
2. Arrange an independent pre-purchase inspection. The article mentions Dr. Cha and similar regulated inspection bodies. The report covered battery health, chassis condition, flood history, cosmetic issues, and other checks. The author says the car passed with minor cosmetic notes and that the arrangement included a deposit refund if it failed inspection.
3. Transfer the vehicle's master account before shipping. The article says remote features, app control, camera access, and basic functions depend on a master account linked to a Chinese phone number.
4. Obtain a working Chinese number that can receive OTPs, set up WeChat, and have the seller transfer the account while the vehicle is still in China.
5. Load accessories into the vehicle before shipping.
6. Arrange after-sales support before the vehicle arrives. The author describes AutoCango coordination, a relationship with a Chinese XPeng-related shop for remote diagnostics and parts support, and local backup with EV World in Abuja.

## Reported risks and lessons
- Confirm English support on the China-spec vehicle. The author's G6 reportedly understands English voice commands but often responds in Mandarin.
- Check real ground clearance and ride behaviour for Nigerian roads. Do not rely only on official figures.
- Treat master-account transfer as necessary for app functionality.
- Use independent inspection because flood damage is a reported risk in parts of China.
- Plan remote diagnostics, parts support, and local service before relying on a grey import.
- Use Chinese-language reviews and tear-downs, including Bilibili, for additional research.
- Be ready to reserve quickly when the right vehicle appears.
