# Email Application Handbook: 500 Templates to Get Applications Noticed

## Source overview

The Email Application Handbook is a recruiter-built reference of subject lines and full email applications, organised by role, for candidates who want their application read rather than skipped. Authored by @halosznn_ (Halo/SMSZ).

## Handbook structure
- 46 pages covering roles from Social Media Manager through Accountant
- Checklist before sending any application
- What NOT to Do section (common mistakes)
- Subject Line Library by Role (30+ roles)
- The Subject Line + Opener Formula
- Industry-Specific Number Prompts (guided fill-ins)
- Tip: if you don't have a number, use a plain outcome instead

## Checklist before sending
- Subject line matches the role tone
- Opening line earns a read (no generic "I'm writing to express")
- Every bracket removed ([Your Name], X years, [Tech Stack], etc.)
- Body states a real company pain point (not generic)
- Includes at least one verified number/result (no invented metrics)
- Mentions CV/portfolio attachment explicitly
- Reads like you, not a copy-pasted template
- CV actually attached (check before sending)
- Portfolio link works and is public/viewable
- File names are professional
- Read it out loud; if stiff, cut a sentence
- Confirm company name and role title are correct (not leftover from different app)
- Sent from a professional email address

## What NOT to Do
- Don't open with filler: "I'm writing to express my interest in the [Role] position..."
- Don't list duties without outcomes: "I managed social media accounts..."
- Don't oversell with no proof: "I'm a hardworking, detail-oriented team player"
- Don't send the same template to every role
- Don't fake numbers (rounding up or inventing metrics)
- Don't ignore the follow-up (one polite follow-up after 5-7 business days is expected)

## Subject Line + Opener Formula

Subject Line Formula: [Tension or contrarian statement about the role's core function]
- Think in terms of: what does this role get blamed for when it goes wrong, or credited for when it goes right?
- Examples: "Nothing Fails at Once in Operations," "Customers Decide Early, Not Late"

Opener Formula (First 2-3 Lines):
1. Name the invisible problem — something true about the role that most people don't say out loud
2. Make it concrete — one or two short, specific consequences (abandoned carts, missed deadlines, silent churn)
3. Pivot to you — "That's what interests me" / "That's the gap I work in" / "That's where my work sits"

Body Formula (3-5 Short Paragraphs):
- Who you are + years of experience + the specific function you work in
- One paragraph of real numbers/results
- One paragraph naming how you think about the role differently ("I don't approach X as ___, I approach it as ___" is a reusable structure)
- CV/portfolio mention
- Closing line: what hiring you actually means for their team

Quick test before sending: if you deleted your name and the company name, could this email be sent for any role in any industry? If yes, it's too generic.

## Industry-Specific Number Prompts (guided fill-ins)

Social Media / Content:
- Followers grown: from ___ to ___
- Engagement rate increase: ___%
- Enquiries/leads generated from content: ___
- Posts/campaigns per month: ___

Graphic Design / Creative:
- Visual assets designed: ___+
- Campaigns contributed to: ___
- Impressions/reach generated: ___
- Industries worked across: ___

Sales / Account Management:
- Deals closed per month/quarter: ___
- Revenue generated/retained: NGN ___ or $___
- Client retention rate: ___%
- Average deal size: ___

Customer Service / CX:
- Enquiries handled per day: ___
- CSAT or resolution rate: ___%
- Average response/resolution time: ___
- Escalations reduced by: ___%

Data / Analytics:
- Datasets/reports handled per week or month: ___
- Decisions/processes influenced by your analysis: ___
- Time saved through automation or dashboards: ___ hours/week

Operations / Admin / EA:
- People/teams supported: ___
- Processes streamlined or built from scratch: ___
- Time saved per week through your systems: ___ hours

Tip: if you genuinely don't have a number yet, don't force one. Use a plain outcome instead ("reduced response time noticeably" rather than a guessed percentage) — interviewers will ask you to explain any number you put in writing.
