# 10 Free University-Built Tools That Beat Paid SaaS (Zotero, Gephi, QGIS, etc.)

X thread: "10 FREE TOOLS BUILT BY UNIVERSITIES THAT BEAT MOST PAID SAAS"

1. Zotero (zotero.org) — George Mason University. Reference manager that replaces EndNote (~$275). One-click source capture, organize thousands of papers, 10,000+ citation styles in Word/Google Docs. EndNote sued to kill it and lost.

2. Stanza (stanfordnlp.github.io/stanza) — Stanford NLP Group (Christopher Manning's lab). Text-analysis toolkit replacing paid APIs. POS tagging, named entity recognition, parsing across 70+ languages.

3. Gephi (gephi.org) — French university researchers. Network visualization for social graphs, citation webs, influence flows. Used by journalists on the Panama Papers.

4. QGIS (qgis.org) — Global academic community. Full geographic mapping platform replacing ArcGIS (thousands per seat). Spatial analysis, professional cartography. Used by universities, governments, NGOs.

5. Jamovi (jamovi.org) — University of Amsterdam. Free statistics platform replacing SPSS ($1,000+/year). Modern interface for statistical analysis.

6. Praat (praat.org) — University of Amsterdam. Speech and sound analysis (pitch, formants, waveforms). Standard tool in linguistics, decades of phonetics research.

7. Cytoscape (cytoscape.org) — Institute for Systems Biology + academic partners. Biological network visualization and interconnected data analysis. Used in genetics and medicine papers.

8. OpenRefine (openrefine.org) — Born at Google, now community/academia run. Data cleaning: find duplicates, fix inconsistencies, reconcile gaps.

9. Zenodo (zenodo.org) — CERN. Free research data/code repository with permanent citable links. Backed by the institution that runs the Large Hadron Collider.

10. Obsidian (obsidian.md) + academic plugins — Used across universities. Free knowledge base with linked notes. Academic plugin ecosystem built by researchers.

Key insight: Companies sell you software and rent the result. Universities build the tool and hand you the keys.
