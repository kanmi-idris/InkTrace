# I turned my old Android Phone into a dashcam, and it works better than the real thing

## Summary
This XDA article describes repurposing an old Android phone into a dashcam with DroidDashCam. The useful pattern is old-device reuse: a spare phone can provide loop recording, configurable storage caps, time-chunked clips, GPS/speed overlays, Bluetooth-triggered auto-start, background recording, Google Maps coexistence, exposure controls, and lens switching, while the main risks are battery drain, heat, battery longevity, mounting safety, and evidence durability. [src-2026-06-03-003]

## Key Claims
- The article argues that an Android phone can outperform some affordable dedicated dashcams by using a better camera sensor, larger screen, and stronger GPS integration. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- DroidDashCam is presented as the enabling app, with continuous loop recording, configurable storage caps, automatic overwriting of old clips, and chunked clips that make incident footage easier to find. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- The setup can burn timestamp, GPS coordinates, current speed, location, and vehicle-registration overlays into footage, which the article frames as potentially useful for insurance or police contexts depending on jurisdiction. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- Recording can auto-start from triggers such as car Bluetooth connection, charger connection, audio-cable connection, or an app-launch button. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- Background recording allows the phone to keep recording while other apps such as Google Maps are used. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- The article recommends using a windshield or dashboard mount, keeping the phone charged, and considering a storage cap such as 32 GB for multi-day loop recording. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- DroidDashCam can switch between phone cameras and day/night exposure behavior; the article treats ultra-wide as the realistic dashcam lens, with telephoto or front-camera use as secondary options. [src-2026-06-03-003]
- The setup is best suited to an older spare phone because long recording sessions create heat, battery drain, and battery-longevity tradeoffs. [src-2026-06-03-003]
