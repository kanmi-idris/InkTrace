# From Loop Engineering to Graph Engineering?

## Summary

Carlos E. Perez's viral essay (1.1M views, retweeted by a Turing Award winner) argues the AI agent field is shifting from single-loop improvement cycles (measure→gap→act→repeat) to graph-of-loops architectures where multiple loops watch, constrain, and correct each other.

## Key Claims

1. **The single loop fails in four specific ways:** Goodhart's law (metric gaming), blindness upward (cannot question the target), conflict (independent loops fight), and measurement decay (sensors detach from reality).

2. **The graph is the emerging answer:** Mature systems (MLOps, well-governed companies, biological regulation) are networks of loops at different speeds with explicit structure in the connections — who watches whom, who can veto whom.

3. **Graphs need anchors:** Adding more loops doesn't buy contact with reality. Some measurements must be externally grounded (bank revenue, physical counts). Some rules must be frozen. The definition of "better" must come from outside.

4. **The durable axis is grounded vs. ungrounded:** The real question isn't loops vs. graphs but whether the improvement machinery stays connected to the reality it claims to improve.
