# Analytical Design, Sparklines, and Layering

## Summary
This source captures a user-supplied analytical-design note extending Tufte-style principles with material associated with *Envisioning Information*, *Visual Explanations*, and *Beautiful Evidence*. It is best understood as a critique and design framework for analytical displays: comparison, causality, multivariate structure, evidence documentation, layering, micro/macro readability, and compact devices like sparklines are treated as the central levers of good information design. [src-2026-05-23-001]

## Key Claims
- The note frames analytical design primarily around showing comparison, causality, multivariate structure, integrated evidence, and content integrity rather than around decorative aesthetics. [src-2026-05-23-001]
- Sparklines are presented as a compact but powerful device for embedding trends directly into prose and tables without resorting to full standalone charts. [src-2026-05-23-001]
- Layering and separation are treated as a central method for making dense displays readable without isolating every element spatially. [src-2026-05-23-001]
- The micro/macro concept is important because it requires displays to communicate both overall pattern and detailed exceptions at different viewing distances. [src-2026-05-23-001]
- The note extends basic Tufte-style critique into a more operational checklist that asks explicitly about comparison, mechanism, documentation, and multiscale readability. [src-2026-05-23-001]
