# What Is Reverse OTP?

## Summary
This source captures a public article that explains reverse OTP as a user-generated verification flow rather than a server-delivered one. Its main value for the vault is conceptual clarity: it makes the pattern legible as an authentication redesign, where the user’s device or SIM originates the verification act instead of merely receiving a code. [src-2026-05-04-001]

## Key Claims
- The article defines reverse OTP as the inverse of common OTP: the user generates and sends the code, while the server verifies it. [src-2026-05-04-001]
- It argues that this can reduce dependence on server-side messaging gateways and potentially shift some operational cost away from the business. [src-2026-05-04-001]
- It presents reverse OTP as especially useful where delivery failures, network delays, or centralized gateway dependence are painful. [src-2026-05-04-001]
- It also emphasizes that reverse OTP does not remove the need for device integrity, encryption, and fraud monitoring. [src-2026-05-04-001]
- A substantial part of the article promotes ReverseOTP.com, so claims about ease of integration, regulatory simplicity, failover, and economics should be read as product-positioning claims rather than independent evidence. [src-2026-05-04-001]
