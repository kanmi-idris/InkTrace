# Details that make interfaces feel better

## Summary
This source captures Jakub Krehel’s article on interface polish. It is best understood as practical design-engineering guidance: a compact collection of implementation-level details around text wrapping, border-radius relationships, interruptible motion, optical alignment, numeric typography, shadows, and image outlining that collectively make interfaces feel more refined. [src-2026-05-27-002]

## Key Claims
- The article’s main claim is cumulative rather than singular: interface quality often comes from many small implementation details compounding together. [src-2026-05-27-002]
- It treats typography and layout details such as balanced text wrapping, tabular numerals, and concentric border radii as high-leverage baseline polish rather than niche embellishments. [src-2026-05-27-002]
- It gives motion-specific guidance that is notably perceptual: interaction animations should be interruptible, entering elements should often be split and staggered, and exit motion should usually be subtler than enter motion. [src-2026-05-27-002]
- It also emphasizes perceptual corrections over raw geometry, especially in optical alignment and the use of layered shadows or faint overlays instead of blunt borders. [src-2026-05-27-002]
- The article is implementation-oriented enough to be reusable by both humans and agents, which is reinforced by the linked open-source `make-interfaces-feel-better` skill. [src-2026-05-27-002]
