# "Creative Thinking" by Claude Shannon

## Summary
Claude Shannon's 1952 Bell Labs talk frames creative work as something more systematic than inspiration. He argues that strong research and invention require training, intelligence, and motivation, then spends most of the lecture on practical problem-solving heuristics: simplify aggressively, search for analogous solved problems, restate the problem from different viewpoints, generalize promising solutions, decompose large leaps into smaller structural steps, and sometimes invert the problem entirely. [src-2026-06-10-014]

## Key Claims
- Shannon says productive research work depends on three prerequisites: training and experience, sufficient intelligence or talent, and a strong motivational drive. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- He describes the motivational component as a mix of curiosity, constructive dissatisfaction with current solutions, and pleasure in elegant results or methods. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- He argues that simplification is a powerful first move because most problems are cluttered with extraneous detail, and solving a stripped-down version can create a path back to the full problem. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- He recommends analogy search: find a similar known problem and map its solved structure back onto the current one. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- He recommends reframing the same problem in many forms to escape mental ruts and expose its real structure. [src-2026-06-10-014]
- He treats generalization, structural decomposition, and inversion as repeatable methods for opening new solution paths in both mathematics and engineering design. [src-2026-06-10-014]
