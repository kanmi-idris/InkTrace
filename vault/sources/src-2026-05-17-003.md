# Magnet-Link Backend Architecture Note

## Summary
This source captures a user-supplied architectural note about what it would actually mean to make magnet links the primary media layer of an application. Its strongest contribution is not basic protocol explanation, but architectural separation: content-addressed storage and decentralized transport are different choices, and a magnet-link-native system drags in seeding, discovery, availability, moderation, encryption, and mobile-reliability constraints that many product teams initially underestimate. [src-2026-05-17-003]

## Key Claims
- The note argues that magnet links should be understood as content identifiers plus decentralized peer-discovery mechanisms, not as a complete transport stack on their own. [src-2026-05-17-003]
- It warns that treating all media as magnet links effectively turns media delivery into a peer-to-peer swarm problem rather than a normal backend-plus-CDN problem. [src-2026-05-17-003]
- The note explicitly distinguishes decentralized transport from content-addressed storage and presents that distinction as the core architectural question. [src-2026-05-17-003]
- It argues that pure torrent-native delivery is a poor fit for private, short-lived, authenticated, low-latency, or mobile-heavy consumer-app workloads. [src-2026-05-17-003]
- The recommended baseline is content-addressed storage with centralized availability, with optional P2P acceleration later rather than magnet-link-native delivery from day one. [src-2026-05-17-003]
- If a team still wants magnet-link-native media, the note says they must plan for torrent generation, persistent seeding, peer discovery, browser-specific WebRTC transport concerns, and hard moderation or encryption problems. [src-2026-05-17-003]
