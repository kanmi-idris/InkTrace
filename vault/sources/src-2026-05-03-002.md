# Patterns for Building AI Agents

## Summary
This source is a Markdown conversion of *Patterns for Building AI Agents* by Sam Bhagwat and Michelle Gienow. Compared with the more foundational “Principles” volume, this one is more production-oriented and pattern-driven: it focuses on capability design, dynamic agents, context engineering, eval design, and agent security controls for systems moving from prototype toward deployment. [src-2026-05-03-002]

## Key Claims
- The book is organized around a set of practical agent-building patterns rather than around first-principles introductions, with sections on configuring agents, engineering context, evaluating responses, and securing autonomy. [src-2026-05-03-002]
- It treats context engineering as a major discipline in its own right, including careful parallelization, context sharing across subagents, compression, and feeding errors back into future execution context. [src-2026-05-03-002]
- It frames eval work as a progression from identifying failure modes and business metrics to building datasets, test suites, SME-labeled data, and production-data evaluation loops. [src-2026-05-03-002]
- Its security section emphasizes risk from autonomous systems and explicitly names controls such as code-execution sandboxing, granular access control, and guardrails. [src-2026-05-03-002]
- The introduction explicitly positions the book as a follow-on to *Principles of Building AI Agents*, shifting from textbook-style guidance toward lessons emerging from people pushing agents into production. [src-2026-05-03-002]
